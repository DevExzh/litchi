## 0617 — the length-changing OLE2 save, baselined; CFB-2 splits by format

Change 0587 ranked CFB-2 (a copy-through writer for length-changing OLE2 saves) at 32 with size `unknown`, because no selector performs such a save, and wrote its own falsification criterion. A retained scratch probe now performs one on five cases through the public editors and attributes it three ways. Natively, by `perf stat` isolation pair on CPU 11 (10/110 iterations, median of 11 repetitions), the container rebuild is **1.07% of cycles** of a length-changing XLS cell-value save on `54016.xls` — inside the +3.29% A/A floor measured in the same window — and **13.25%** and **29.99%** of a DOC paragraph save on `FloatingPictures.doc` (335 KB) and `NoHeadFoot.doc` (26 KB), of which 12.69% and 26.04% of the whole save is work proportional to streams the edit never touched. The criterion is therefore met for XLS and not for DOC, and the deciding quantity is not the container at all: it is the **unchanged-stream share**, a property of the format's stream topology, measured at 4.8% on the XLS fixture (one `Workbook` stream is 95.2% of the artifact and every edit rewrites it) against 81.4% on the DOC one (a 255 KB `Data` stream and seven `ObjectPool` streams are never rewritten) and 16.9% on `45543.ppt`. Two by-products rank higher than CFB-2 itself. On XLS, 64.8% of the commit's instructions are `litchi-xls` and `litchi-biff` re-parsing the workbook during the commit readback — `BTreeMap<(u32,u32),Cell>::insert` 11.74%, `worksheet::add_cell` 10.60%, `biff::stream::Records::next` 8.48% — which is 0587's **XLS-9** at rank 31, now sized at 2.22× the open in cycles and the larger, cheaper, lower-risk item. On PPT, whole-artifact SHA-256 is 67.18% of callgrind instructions, which is **DOC-1** at rank 2, not CFB-2. The one CFB-2 advantage that does not depend on format is retention: the container rebuild alone peaks at **5.2× to 5.9× the artifact size** on every case, and on the DOC fixture 85.8% of that peak is the untouched streams. Instrument caveat for the rest of this queue: 85% of the container leg is `rep movsb`/`rep stosb`, so callgrind reported 27.52% where the hardware measures 13.25%. `performance_claim: none`; no production change. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded. [Record and limitations](0617-cfb-copy-through-writer-design.md); [retained evidence](results/change-0617/README.md).

## For `GOAL_AUDIT.md`

## 0617 — `docs/GOAL.md`'s "unchanged-stream or unchanged-sector copy-through on save" designed, priced and blocked on an ADR

The LEGACY CFB-SPECIFIC WORK list asks for "unchanged-stream or unchanged-sector copy-through on save". This change writes that design out in full — a fourth `litchi-cfb` publication path alongside `OleWriter`, `SequentialOleWriter` and the same-length overlay, built by generalizing `ComposedOverlaySource` from spans-over-a-source to spans-plus-an-appended-tail, with a planner that rebuilds only the FAT, DIFAT, MiniFAT, directory and changed streams and copies every other directory byte verbatim — and states its nine invariants, its seven admission gates and its seven-part parity gate. It is **not** implemented, for two independent reasons. First, measurement: on XLS the container rebuild is 1.07% of a save in cycles, inside this host's floor, so the design would buy nothing there; on DOC it is 13.25-29.99%, so it would. Second, and blocking regardless: **no ADR addresses the physical sector layout of a saved CFB.** ADR 0005 authorizes the mechanism ("Preserve-mode save raw-copies unchanged compressed ZIP entries or CFB streams when possible") and ADR 0008 names sector *size* in its preservation list, but nothing says whether a saved file may reuse a `FREESECT`, must append, or must preserve placement. All three policies preserve every logical byte; append-only grows `FloatingPictures.doc` by about 70 KiB on every save, and free-sector reuse relocates bytes a byte-comparing differ can see. Choosing one in code would be making preservation policy inside a performance change, so the record asks for the clarification and recommends reusing only the sectors the operation itself released. Two audit rows this change opens: the unchanged-stream share, which decides whether CFB-2 is worth a rank at all, is measured on five fixtures and ranges 2.3-81.4%, and a census over all 212 OLE2 fixtures is the cheapest next measurement; and no `.ppt` fixture in this repository can reach a length-changing shape-text edit at all, so that landed capability has no corpus. No latency, RSS, allocation or throughput improvement is claimed by this batch. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded.

## 0617 — what a length-changing OLE2 save actually costs

Change 0617 builds the baseline change 0587 recorded as missing and declines to implement the item it was meant to justify. Five length-changing edit-and-save cases were run through the public editors — an XLS string-cell edit on `54016.xls`, DOC paragraph replacements on `FloatingPictures.doc` and `NoHeadFoot.doc`, a slide removal on `45543.ppt` and a shape-text edit on a deck authored by `litchi_ppt::writer::Writer` — each with four operations so that isolation pairs can difference them. Native `perf stat` on CPU 11 gives, per operation: XLS commit 134,973,045 cycles against its own open's 60,867,372, with the container rebuild at 1,444,903 (1.07%); DOC 4,956,372 and 359,673 with the container at 656,574 (13.25%) and 107,855 (29.99%); PPT 6,501,647 with the container at 472,088 (7.26%). Instructions agree to within a few points on every row. The A/A control, the same leg run as both legs interleaved with the rest, is +3.29% on cycles and −0.108% on instructions. Deterministic counts sit under all of it: the XLS save leaves 46,458 of 973,636 stream bytes untouched, the DOC save 264,190 of 324,540. A counting allocator in an isolated binary puts the container rebuild's peak at 5.23× to 5.87× the artifact size on every case. Gates on the clean worktree at the base commit: `cargo fmt --all --check` clean, Clippy clean on `litchi-cfb` and `litchi-ole-common` with all targets, `cargo test -p litchi-cfb` 322 + 13 + 6 passed plus 12 doctests with 1 ignored, `cargo test -p litchi-ole-common` 129 + 14 + 2 + 8 + 5 passed, 0 failed anywhere, and `cargo doc` clean on both. One defect found on the way and reported rather than fixed: `OleWriter::write_to` produces different bytes on every run when a document has two or more explicitly created storages, because it iterates a `HashSet` to assign directory SIDs — twelve runs in twelve processes gave twelve distinct digests of the same-length output. No production code changed; `performance_claim: none`. [Change and limitations](0617-cfb-copy-through-writer-design.md); [retained evidence](results/change-0617/README.md).

## For `ADR_COMPLIANCE.md`

## 0617 — a design stated against its boundaries, and one ADR 0006 defect found

Nothing was implemented, so no ADR boundary moved and no accepted hash changed. The frozen copy-through design is recorded against the boundaries it would have to hold. ADR 0005 supplies its authority — "Preserve-mode save raw-copies unchanged compressed ZIP entries or CFB streams when possible" — and is otherwise untouched: every mandatory structural validation runs, because the design's own proof is a reopen through `SharedOleFile`, which is where `validate_stream_allocations` and `validate_physical_sector_layout` live; bounded resources improve from O(file) to O(changed streams plus buffer); atomic publication is unchanged; and the admission gates return a typed capability refusal *before output*, which is ADR 0005's own requirement for a source that cannot be published through a proven preservation plan. ADR 0006's preservation default is strengthened rather than weakened: untouched streams become byte-identical by proof — a readback of every untouched stream against the source, bounded by a limit — instead of by reconstruction. ADR 0026's directory binding holds by construction, because each directory entry's 64 bytes are copied verbatim and only `start_sector` and `stream_size` of changed entries are overwritten, so no SID moves, no red-black link or colour is re-derived, and the ADR's "state bits and timestamps are intentionally not fabricated" holds because neither field is written. The 0175 policy — the composed artifact is reopened through the normal CFB parser before a sink can observe a byte — and the complete fingerprint ledger frozen by 0103, 0143 and 0175 are carried through unchanged, including the owned-immutable specialization, which is a property of the source rather than of the composition. The one boundary the design cannot settle for itself is physical sector layout, which no ADR addresses; it is raised as an explicit clarification request with three candidate policies rather than decided in code, and the design stays frozen until it is answered. Separately, one existing ADR 0006 violation was found and is reported for the owner, not fixed: ADR 0006 requires "Serialization is deterministic unless a `Clock`, actor identity, or cryptographic RNG is explicitly supplied", and `OleWriter::write_to` iterates `self.storages: HashSet<Vec<String>>` to assign directory SIDs in insertion order, so a document with two or more explicitly created storages serializes to different bytes in different processes — twelve distinct digests in twelve runs at eight storages, one digest in twelve runs at one. `performance_claim: none`. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded.
