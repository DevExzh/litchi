## 0612 — the XLS globals skip fires on real fixtures and still loses; XLS-6 declined, and the coefficient that ranked it corrected

The 0587 queue ranked XLS-6 (skip never-interpreted globals payloads, frame once) at 24 on a retained figure of "about 30% of the flagship open Ir" against "+46 requests", and singled it out from the untestable worksheet gate of change 0568 because *its* density gate could fire on a real fixture. Both halves are now measured, and they point opposite ways. The gate **does** fire: a per-record rule at 1 KiB reaches **19 of 123** modellable `.xls`/`.xlt` fixtures, and on `ConditionalFormattingSamples.xls` a source-backed open falls from **565,201 to 82,867 read bytes, −85.34%**, for +48 positional reads and +56 source observations, with `WithCustomViews.xls` and `54016.xls` identical on every counter, every operation, both source modes. The change is nevertheless **falsified**: natively that open is **+5.54% in cycles on an owned in-memory source and +17.22% on a file source**, +8.94% in instructions, and +5.91%/+6.45% and +16.93%/+16.35% at p50 in paired wall clock in both directions, against an A/A floor **under 1%** measured in the same window. Callgrind says the opposite — −15.33% — and the reconciliation is the standing caution of this program: **412,826 of the 356,962-instruction "saving" is `__memcpy_avx_unaligned_erms` priced one instruction per byte**, and callgrind's delta outside `memcpy` and `memset` (+84,374) has the same sign and four fifths of the magnitude of the native instruction *rise* (+105,415). The deeper correction is to the sizing: change 0574's **53.4 ns per KiB** of globals payload, a corpus-regression coefficient, predicted **−19.6 µs** net for this schedule (−25.2 µs of bytes against +5.6 µs of requests) where the measured open moved **+4,300 ns** on an owned source and **+15,410 ns** on a file source; priced instead at change 0604's measured 0.0165 cycles per byte for this very buffer, the skipped bytes are about **1.7 µs**, some fifteen times smaller — because a cross-fixture regression attributes to bytes everything that scales with them, none of which is removed by not reading them. Three further findings for the queue: change 0568's cumulative running-mean gate is **inert** here (fires on 0 of 123, saves 3.0% of bytes against 59.0% for a per-record rule plus a fill-target reset), because the skippable mass is one dense run inside an otherwise sparse stream; a per-record kind test in the globals frame loop costs **16.8–17.5 instructions per record whether or not it fires**, which is the whole of `54016.xls`'s +1.08%; and **change 0574 opportunity 3 (a retained globals cursor), ranked and never measured, regresses on its own** at +10.90%/+9.37% of the flagship open in cycles, because `SharedOleStreamCursor`'s per-fill state machine costs +150,272 Ir against the −97,145 it saves now that `read_stream_range_hinted` carries a chain hint. `performance_claim: none`; no production change. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded. [Record and limitations](0612-xls-skip-uninterpreted-globals-design.md); [retained evidence](results/change-0612/README.md).

## For `GOAL_AUDIT.md`

## 0612 — a `docs/GOAL.md` step-1 candidate closed by measuring the cost it trades into, not the work it removes

`docs/GOAL.md`'s optimization order puts "eliminate unnecessary work" first and "unnecessary I/O" second, and 75.30% of the globals bytes a source-backed XLS open reads are payloads no consumer interprets — by byte volume the largest remaining instance of both on this path. This record establishes that removing them is not free, because the same order requires "bounded read-ahead and range coalescing based on measured access patterns" and avoiding "request amplification from many tiny remote reads", and the skip trades directly against both: it converts one large read into more, smaller reads, and each read on a `from_path` workbook is a `pread64` **and** an `fstat` freshness observation, 29 rising to 85 on one flagship open. Measured, the trade loses on every source kind this repository can build — +5.54% of the open in cycles on an owned in-memory source, +17.22% on a file source — and the only source kind on which it could win, a range or remote source, does not exist in the corpus, so its side of the trade is modelled from change 0564's 116 ns per request and **never measured**. That gap is the audit row this record leaves open, and it is the same row change 0587 opened for the whole read path. Two further audit rows are narrowed rather than closed: `xls_source_attribution` still has no full-text, no all-cells and no `from_path`-observation selector, so the scenarios this design would make *worse* cannot be timed (both re-enter the same open, so both would be worse, not better); and the design's own admission gates — a `ParsedGlobals` differential over all 123 modellable fixtures, error identity on the three encrypted refusals, and paired timing on dense and sparse globals in both directions — are frozen in the record so a later batch inherits them rather than re-deriving them. The decision rules were applied as written: the schedule was modelled before anything was built, three measurement scaffolds were built, measured and reverted rather than argued about, the design was priced in its **best** form (skip plus retained cursor, which is worse still at +18.68%/+15.99%), and the result that contradicted the ranking was reported rather than folded into a mean. `docs/GOAL.md` rule 10 is untouched (no `unsafe`; `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` stands in `litchi-xls`) and rule 12 is untouched (no limit, budget, cancellation point or malformed-input defence is weakened, because nothing changed). No latency, RSS, allocation or throughput improvement is claimed by this batch. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded.

## For `REPORT.md`

## 0612 — what the XLS globals skip saves, what it costs, and why the design is frozen rather than landed

Change 0612 prices change 0574 opportunity 2 and declines it. A schedule model built from a pure-arithmetic CFB/BIFF8 walk — sharing no code with `litchi-xls`, so it is an oracle rather than a restatement — replays today's `parse_globals` fill schedule byte-for-byte over all 126 `.xls` and `.xlt` fixtures and five candidate schedules beside it; it reproduces 0574's closure share (75.30% here against 75.08%, the difference being the three encrypted fixtures 0574 counted) and predicts the flagship's measured byte saving of 482,334 **to the byte**. It also corrects the survey's picture of the flagship's skippable mass: not "about 261 records, about 2 KiB per record" but **64 payloads at or above 1 KiB holding 524,583 bytes**, almost all at the 8,224-byte BIFF maximum — one `MsoDrawingGroup` of 16,448 bytes in two frames plus about 62 maximal `Continue` records, each attributed to the record its run continues. Four legs were then built from one worktree differing only in `crates/litchi-xls/src/workbook/source.rs` — the base, a scaffold that changes only *which stream bytes are fetched* while keeping the buffer's shape and every semantic output byte-identical, a scaffold that swaps the range reader for a retained cursor, and both together — and measured pinned to CPU 8 under `setarch -R`. Thirty-six deterministic counter cells are the control: the flagship open goes 53 → 101 reads, 565,201 → 82,867 bytes, 29 → 85 observations, while `WithCustomViews.xls` and `54016.xls` are identical on every counter in both source modes, and the harness's independent eager-parser oracle returns the same worksheet names and the same selected cell on every leg. Callgrind isolation pairs put the flagship open at −15.33%; native `perf stat`, median of five repetitions, puts it at **+5.54% of cycles on an owned source and +17.22% on a file source**, with instructions **rising** 8.94% — the difference is 412,826 instructions of `rep movsb` priced per byte, and callgrind's delta outside `memcpy` and `memset` has the same sign and four fifths of the magnitude of the native rise. The cost sites are named rather than inferred: `read_stream_range_hinted` resolves the CFB directory entry **by path on every call**, so 48 extra reads cost +33,656 Ir in `directory_name_data`, +16,016 in `find_entry` and +9,352 in the `SmallVec` path vector, plus +20,055 in the `realloc` family, +10,514 in the gate test and +12,168 in the harness's own per-read timers, which are named and sized rather than subtracted. A paired `A1 B1 B2 A2` wall clock of 400 samples per round confirms it in both directions (+5.91%/+6.45% owned, +16.93%/+16.35% file) against a same-binary floor of −0.98% to +0.47% at p50 measured in the same window with a byte-identical B leg. The design is frozen unimplemented, together with one contract the survey did not name: `RecordRef::offset()` feeds `SharedStringSstSegment::source_offset`, which the resolve path uses with `skip_to` to fetch a shared string's bytes at query time, so a compacted globals buffer must carry each retained record's **stream** offset separately or every shared string on every fixture would be read from the wrong position. Gates on the clean worktree at the base commit: `cargo fmt --all --check` clean, Clippy clean on `litchi-xls` with all targets, `cargo test -p litchi-xls` 72 binaries with 1,390 passed, 0 failed and 1 ignored, `cargo doc` clean. No production code changed; `performance_claim: none`. [Change and limitations](0612-xls-skip-uninterpreted-globals-design.md); [retained evidence](results/change-0612/README.md).

## 0612 — no boundary moved; the three contracts of change 0565 the frozen design would have had to renegotiate

Nothing was implemented, so no ADR boundary moved and no accepted hash changed; the frozen design is recorded against the boundaries it would have to hold. ADR 0005's open-time obligation — container, relationship, security and mandatory structural validation at open, semantic payloads lazily — **admits** the skip rather than straining against it, and change 0565's "buffered but never published" contract extends to bytes never fetched, strictly narrowing what is ever resident: a `FilePass` framed after the exact prologue may still leave payload bytes in a fill, and fewer fills means fewer such bytes. The exact prologue is untouched, so an encrypted workbook is still refused in two reads with no `FilePass` payload byte requested, and `max_global_bytes` keeps its typed variant, its `resource`, its `observed` and its plus-four boundary — what changes is its *meaning*, from an upper bound on bytes read to a strictly looser bound on bytes retained. Three contracts of change 0565 would have to be renegotiated and each is stated rather than left to be discovered: the covering property "every globals byte is read exactly once, over-read exactly zero", asserted by `source_backed_open_reads_each_global_byte_once` and by a facade test in `crates/litchi/src/sheet/workbook.rs`, becomes "every *retained* globals byte, and no read begins inside a skipped payload", which is still a change detector and still stronger than the pre-0565 shape; a read failure whose only evidence lies in sectors nothing interprets would no longer surface at open, though every structural defence survives because the allocation chain is still walked past those sectors, so ADR 0006's ownership, overlap, chain-consistency and length checks are untouched; and error precedence moves **back** one step — change 0565 accepted that an I/O failure at a later offset can pre-empt a limit error of an earlier record in the same fill, and removing that read removes that precedence, which is the opposite direction from the accepted change and closer to the pre-0565 order. Two further compliance facts are worth recording because they are easy to assume wrongly: **no skipped payload is validated today** — both consumers reach every skipped kind through a `_ => {}` arm — so the skip removes no check and weakens no defence; and ADR 0005's freshness clause is engaged in the *costly* direction, since the skip raises source-version observations from 29 to 85 per flagship open, each an `fstat` on a `from_path` workbook, which is the same per-observation cost item XLS-4 of change 0587 names. ADR 0003's transaction boundary and ADR 0006's preservation clause are not engaged: this is the source-backed read path, which publishes no bytes, and no output byte changes. `performance_claim: none`. OLE2/OOXML remain active; ODF is deferred until completion and iWork excluded.
